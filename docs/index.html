From this row to its noise weights

Seven steps from the row above to the weights w that turn noise into a change of 𝒮 , and to why S23's residual radius is so much larger than the scatter those weights predict.

Project the row onto the DCT basis:

\[\vc=\Cm^T\vi .\]

\(\Cm\) is orthonormal and its columns are the eigenvectors of \(\Dn^T\Dn\) (S23 Eqs. 12–14), so every operator that follows is diagonal here.

White noise puts the same variance \(\sigma_n^2\) in every mode. get_kc sets \(k_c\) = – where the burst's \(|c_k|\), maximised over trial DMs, falls to the mean maximum of the noise-only modes \(k>N/2\). The burst still carries power above \(\sigma_n\) beyond \(k_c\). Its points split into odd and even \(k\): the burst sits near the middle of the series, so alternate modes sample the cosine and the sine of each component's phase (step 7 shows the consequence).

The noise level is measured from this same on-pulse row, in modes where the burst has no power:

\[\hat\sigma_n=1.4826\,\mathrm{median}_{k>N/4}\lvert c_k\rvert .\]

this row: \(\hat\sigma_n\) = –

Three diagonal filters. The low-pass (S23 Eq. 16):

\[\Lm=\mathbf{D}_{\rm filter}=\mathrm{diag}(f_k),\qquad f_k=[1+(k/k_c)^{2O}]^{-1}.\]

The high-pass, the amplitude response of \(\Dn\):

\[\Hm=\boldsymbol\Lambda^{1/2}=\mathrm{diag}(h_k),\qquad h_k=2\sin[(k-1)\pi/2N].\]

Combined, the band-pass of S23 Fig. 2:

\[\F=\Hm\Lm .\]

S23's residual \(\Dn\Sm\Di\) is weighted by \(h_kf_k(1-f_k)\), dashed. It peaks at \(k_c\), where \(f_k=\tfrac12\) and, by the choice of \(k_c\), burst and noise are comparable.

Code names: \(f_k\) = fL, \(h_k\) = hp, \(h_kf_k\) = combined.

The smoothed row \(\ti=\Sm\vi\) and its first difference \(\Dn\ti\). The structure parameter is the length of that vector (S23 Eq. 18):

\[\SP=\nrm{\Dn\ti}=\nrm{\F\vc}.\]

With \(\vi=\vs+\vn\) the vector splits exactly:

\[\Dn\ti=\va+\ve,\qquad \va=\Dn\Sm\vs,\quad \ve=\Dn\Sm\vn .\]

In this mock \(\vs\) is known, so both parts can be measured.

this row: 𝒮 = –\(\nrm{\va}\) = –\(\nrm{\ve}\) = –

Expanding the norm of \(\va+\ve\) with \(\ah=\va/\nrm{\va}\):

\[\SP=\nrm{\va}+\ah^T\ve+\frac{\nrm{\ve}^2-(\ah^T\ve)^2}{2\nrm{\va}}+\mathcal{O}(e^3).\]

Only \(\ah^T\ve\) is linear in the noise. Moving the operators onto \(\ah\):

\[\ah^T\ve=\vn^T\vw,\qquad \vw=\Sm\Dn^T\ah .\]

Read the strip left to right: difference the smoothed burst to get \(\ah\), difference it back with \(\Dn^T\), smooth again. So \(\vw\) is minus a smoothed second derivative of the burst: positive on the peaks, negative on the flanks. Noise that lifts a peak sharpens the burst and raises 𝒮 ; noise on a flank broadens it and lowers 𝒮 .

In the DCT basis, with \(\ah\) estimated from the smoothed data (solid):

\[\vw=\Cm\F\vu,\qquad \vu=\F\Lm\vc/\nrm{\F\Lm\vc}.\]

The dotted curve uses the noiseless burst, which only the mock knows:

\[\vu=\F\vc_s/\nrm{\F\vc_s}.\]

Visualisation only: \(\vw=\Sm\Dn^T\ah\) applied one operator at a time. x is time (μs), the same window as the panel below; y is dimensionless, since \(\ah\) is a unit vector (\(\sum_t\hat a_t^2=1\)) and \(\Dn\), \(\Sm\) are unitless. So \(\vw\) carries no units and \(\vw^T\vn\) is in units of I, like 𝒮 .

The linear noise term is a weighted sum of the noise samples, with weights fixed by the burst:

\[\ah^T\ve=\vw^T\vn=\sum_t w_tn_t .\]

Its mean is zero and its variance is

\[\sigma^2=\vw^T\Cov(\vn)\,\vw ,\]

which for white noise is (replacing S23 Eq. 19)

\[\sigma=\sigma_n\nrm{\vw}=\sigma_n\nrm{\F\vu}.\]

With self-noise, using the local variance \(\sigma_t^2\) of the \(k>N/4\) band (bottom panel):

\[\sigma^2=\sum_tw_t^2\sigma_t^2 .\]

The panels use the true \(\ah\), so the running sum ends at the first-order term of this draw:

Split S23's residual the same way as the data:

\[\Dn\Sm\Di=\dvs+\dvn,\]

\[\dvs=\Dn\Sm(\vs-\Sm\vs),\qquad \dvn=\Dn\Sm(\vn-\Sm\vn).\]

\(\dvs\) is the burst structure above \(k_c\) that smoothing removes, identical in every draw, and \(\dvn\) is its noise.

Each grey dot is one Monte-Carlo draw at ΔDM = 0: the true noise vector \(\ve=\Dn\Sm\vn\) split into its component along \(\ah\) (horizontal) and its length perpendicular to \(\ah\) (vertical), on equal scales. It is long but nearly perpendicular to the structure, where it changes 𝒮  only at second order; only the horizontal spread is an uncertainty. Each red dot is S23's residual \(\Dn\Sm\Di\) from the same draw, split the same way. Lengths are non-negative, so the space below the \(\ah\) axis only holds the vectors' labels.

For isotropic noise \(\langle\cos^2\theta\rangle=1/N\). \(\dvn\) is band-limited near \(k_c\), and the count is

\[N_{\rm eff}=\langle\nrm{\dvn}^2\rangle/\sigma^2,\]

so the residual noise radius exceeds σ by \(\sqrt{N_{\rm eff}}\).

With \(\Dn\Sm\Di=\dvs+\dvn\) (step 6) the cross term \(2\dvs^T\dvn\) averages to zero, so

\[\langle\nrm{\Dn\Sm\Di}^2\rangle=\nrm{\dvs}^2+\langle\nrm{\dvn}^2\rangle=\langle\nrm{\dvn}^2\rangle(1+\Gamma),\]

with the burst-to-noise power in the residual

\[\Gamma=\nrm{\dvs}^2/\langle\nrm{\dvn}^2\rangle .\]

Dividing by σ² and using \(N_{\rm eff}=\langle\nrm{\dvn}^2\rangle/\sigma^2\) from step 6,

\[\frac{\langle\nrm{\Dn\Sm\Di}^2\rangle}{\sigma^2}=N_{\rm eff}(1+\Gamma).\]

\(\dvs\) is the same in every draw, so it is not an uncertainty, yet it sits at \(k\simeq k_c\), where the cut was placed. The graph splits the squared lengths by DCT mode, where (S23 Eq. 17)

\[d_{s,k}=h_kf_k(1-f_k)\,c_{s,k},\qquad d_{n,k}=h_kf_k(1-f_k)\,\nu_k .\]

Odd and even \(k\) are drawn separately: the burst sits near the middle of the series, so alternate modes sample the cosine and the sine of each component's phase, and its two components, 50 μs apart, beat with a period of \(2N/\Delta t\approx82\) modes.

From the weights to σDM

The DM error comes from how the noise term changes with trial DM. Five steps: what a DM step does to the weights, why their time derivative appears, the slope noise it produces, how a slope moves a parabola's peak, and the result against the Monte Carlo. Weights here are those of the selected realisation at its peak row.

To first order

\[\SP(d)\simeq\nrm{\va(d)}+e_\parallel(d),\]

where the noise term at trial DM \(d\) is the component of the noise vector along \(\ah\) (step 4.4),

\[e_\parallel(d)=\ah^T\ve_d=\vw^T\vn_d ,\]

and the noise row at trial DM \(d\) is

\[\vn_d(t)=\sum_{\nu}x_{\nu}(t-d\,\tau_{\nu}),\]

with \(\nu_{\rm ref}\) chosen so that \(\sum_{\nu}\tau_{\nu}=0\) (step 2 shows why this is allowed). Move the delay from the noise onto the weights:

\[e_\parallel(d_0+\Delta d)=\sum_{\nu}\sum_t w(t+\tau_{\nu}\Delta d)\,x_{\nu}(t).\]

The noise in each channel stays put; a DM step slides that channel's copy of the weights by \(\tau_{\nu}\Delta d\). Low-frequency channels slide furthest.

For small steps

\[w(t+\tau_{\nu}\Delta d)-w(t)\simeq\tau_{\nu}\Delta d\,\dot w(t),\]

so the change of weight on each noise sample is the time derivative of the weights, scaled by that channel's delay.

A delay shared by every channel moves signal and noise together and leaves 𝒮  unchanged, so \(\nu_{\rm ref}\) can be chosen to make \(\sum_{\nu}\tau_{\nu}=0\) and only the spread of the delays counts:

\[e_\parallel'=\sum_{\nu}\tau_{\nu}\,\dot{\vw}^T\vx_{\nu},\quad \Var\,e_\parallel'=\sigma_n^2\sigma_\tau^2\nrm{\dot{\vw}}^2 ,\]

with

\[\sigma_\tau^2=\Var_{\nu}(\tau_{\nu}).\]

In the DCT basis

\[\nrm{\dot{\vw}}=\nrm{\Om\F\vu},\qquad \omega_k=\pi(k-1)/N\Delta t .\]

\[\{d:\SP_{\rm peak}-\SP_d\le\kappa\,\sigma_{{\rm peak},d}\}\]
